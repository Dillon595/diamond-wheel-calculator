    About This Calculator

    This grinding wheel calculator helps engineers verify wheel parameters, surface speeds, and recommended grinding conditions for diamond and CBN superabrasive wheels. It calculates surface speed in m/s and ft/min, material removal rate (MRR), grinding power, G-ratio, and chip thickness.

    The tool provides safety warnings for overspeed conditions and incorrect abrasive-material pairings, making it useful for both initial wheel selection and production parameter verification.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I calculate grinding wheel surface speed?

    Surface speed (m/s) = π × Wheel Diameter (m) × RPM ÷ 60. For example, a 150mm diameter wheel at 3000 RPM has a surface speed of π × 0.150 × 3000 ÷ 60 = 23.6 m/s.

    What is MRR (Material Removal Rate)?

    MRR = Depth of Cut × Wheel Width × Table Speed. It measures how much material is removed per minute, typically in cm³/min. Higher MRR means faster grinding but may affect surface finish.

    What is the maximum safe speed for grinding wheels?

    Maximum safe speed depends on bond type: resin bond ≤35 m/s, vitrified bond ≤60 m/s, metal bond ≤30 m/s, electroplated ≤40 m/s. Exceeding these limits risks wheel breakage. These are typical industry values — always verify against the maximum operating speed printed on the specific grinding wheel.

    Should I use flood coolant for diamond wheel grinding?

    Flood coolant is recommended for most diamond and CBN grinding operations. It extends wheel life, improves surface finish, and prevents thermal damage. Dry grinding is generally not recommended for resin bond wheels.

    How do I convert SFPM to RPM?

    To convert wheel surface speed to spindle speed: RPM = (SFPM × 12) ÷ (π × wheel diameter in inches). For example, 4638 SFPM on a 6-inch wheel gives RPM = (4638 × 12) ÷ (π × 6) ≈ 2950.

    What is G-ratio in grinding?

    G-ratio is the ratio of workpiece material removed to grinding wheel volume worn away. A G-ratio of 30 means 30 cm³ of material removed per 1 cm³ of wheel wear. Higher G-ratio means longer wheel life and lower wheel cost per part. Typical values range from under 10 for conventional abrasives to well over 100 for metal-bond superabrasive wheels.

    Values provided by this calculator are typical industry estimates for preliminary setup only. Always verify against your machine specifications and the maximum operating speed stamped on the grinding wheel (per EN 13218 / ANSI B7.1). Run a trial grind before production.
